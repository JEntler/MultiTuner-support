Use the comparison while you tune

  1. Check your starting tuning. The changes assume your open strings already match the “From” notes. If you do not know, start by checking Standard.
  2. Play one open string at a time. Mute the others. A tuner can center on a different note, so check the target note before centering its pitch.
  3. Make small changes and recheck all six. Changing string tension can affect strings you already tuned. Peg direction depends on your instrument.

Lower tunings can feel loose or buzz; Drop C may need different strings or a setup. If a string feels unusually tight, stop and check the note and octave.

Eight guitar tunings, at a glance.

Every note below is an open string, shown from thickest to thinnest. A half step is one semitone; a whole step is two.

Standard

E2 · A2 · D3 · G3 · B3 · E4

The usual six-string guitar tuning: E–A–D–G–B–E.

Drop D

D2 · A2 · D3 · G3 · B3 · E4

From Standard, lower only the thickest string from E to D. The other five strings stay the same.

Half step down (E♭ Standard)

E♭2 · A♭2 · D♭3 · G♭3 · B♭3 · E♭4

Every string is one semitone below Standard. E♭–A♭–D♭–G♭–B♭–E♭ is also written D♯–G♯–C♯–F♯–A♯–D♯.

D Standard

D2 · G2 · C3 · F3 · A3 · D4

Every string is one whole step below Standard. Unlike Drop D, all six strings change.

Drop C

C2 · G2 · C3 · F3 · A3 · D4

From Standard, lower the thickest string two whole steps and the other five one whole step. From D Standard, lower only the thickest string a whole step.

Open G

D2 · G2 · D3 · G3 · B3 · D4

From Standard, lower strings 6, 5 and 1 a whole step. The open strings form a G major chord.

Open D

D2 · A2 · D3 · F♯3 · A3 · D4

D–A–D–F♯–A–D. Compared with DADGAD, only the third string changes: G goes down to F♯ (also written G♭).

DADGAD

D2 · A2 · D3 · G3 · A3 · D4

From Standard, lower strings 6, 2 and 1 a whole step. The third string stays G, which distinguishes DADGAD from Open D.

Common points of confusion

Is Drop D the same as D Standard?

No. Drop D is D–A–D–G–B–E. D Standard is D–G–C–F–A–D. From Standard, Drop D changes one string; D Standard changes all six.

Is E♭ Standard the same as D♯ Standard?

Yes, in equal temperament they use the same pitches with different note names. Switch the ♯ option above if your tuner uses sharps.

Do I need a paid tuner to use these notes?

A chromatic tuner can follow the notes you play. MultiTuner includes chromatic mode and all its built-in presets free, within two saved tuners. Creating or editing custom target notes requires Premium; E♭ Standard and Drop C are custom targets rather than built-in presets.
