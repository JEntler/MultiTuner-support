Standard instrument tunings you can hear

Hear the open strings of a guitar, violin, ukulele, koto and more. Play a whole tuning in string order, or tap one note to hear it on its own.

Sampled reference sounds at A4 = 440 Hz. The number beside a note is its octave. The smaller number above it identifies the string or course.

Guitar

Six-string guitar · Standard

The open low E and high E are two octaves apart.

Yamaha’s standard guitar tuning

Seven-string guitar · Standard

The six upper strings keep standard guitar tuning. String 7 adds B1.

Strandberg’s seven- and eight-string tunings

Eight-string guitar · Standard

String 8 adds F♯1 below the seventh string’s B1.

Strandberg’s seven- and eight-string tunings

Ukulele

Ukulele · Standard high G

Standard for soprano, concert and many tenor ukuleles. String 4 is higher than the open C and E strings.

Kala’s ukulele tuning guide

Ukulele · Low G

A dedicated low-G fourth string puts G3 one octave below high G. The other strings stay the same.

Kala’s ukulele tuning guide

Baritone ukulele · Standard

The same open pitches as strings 4 through 1 on a standard guitar.

Kala’s ukulele tuning guide

Electric bass

Four-string bass · Standard

The same open pitches as a four-string upright bass in orchestral tuning.

Yamaha’s electric bass tunings

Five-string bass · Low B

The common low-B setup extends the four-string bass downward. Some five-string basses add a high C instead.

Yamaha’s electric bass tunings

Six-string bass · Standard

Low B and high C extend the familiar E–A–D–G set in both directions.

Yamaha’s six-string bass note chart

Bowed strings

Violin · Standard

The four open strings rise in fifths and match the pitches of a mandolin’s four courses.

Yamaha’s violin and viola comparison

Viola · Standard

The upper three strings match the violin’s G3, D4 and A4. The viola extends down to C3.

Yamaha’s violin and viola comparison

Cello · Standard

The same open-string letters as the viola, one octave lower.

Yamaha’s cello guide

Upright bass · Orchestral

The four strings rise in fourths. The notes shown are sounding pitches. Double-bass parts are usually written an octave higher.

Philharmonia’s double bass guide

Mandolin, banjo and koto

Mandolin · Standard

Mandolin has four courses, or pairs. Both strings in a pair are tuned to the same pitch.

Classical Mandolin Society’s notation guide

Five-string banjo · Open G

The short fifth string is the high G drone. It sounds higher than every other open string.

Deering’s banjo tuning guide

13-string koto · Hirajōshi in D

In this classical setting, string 1 shares D4 with string 5. Some modern pieces use D3 for string 1. Movable bridges set the pitches.

European Shakuhachi Society’s Hirajōshi notes
